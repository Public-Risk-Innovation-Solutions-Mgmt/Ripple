Your Pool has been created. Here is how the game works.

## The yearly cycle

Every year follows the same steps:

1. Review the reports from the year just closed.
2. Make your decisions for the coming year.
3. Advance the year. Claims, investments and events unfold, and the results come back.
4. Repeat for as many years as the game runs.

There is no single score. The challenge is keeping coverage affordable for members while keeping the Pool strong
enough to pay its claims, in good years and bad. Some decisions pay off quickly; others show their effect only years
later.

## Your decisions

Some decisions are set for each coverage line; others apply to the whole Pool.

**Funding, by line.** How much to charge against the losses you expect. Funding at Expected charges what claims are
expected to cost. Funding higher builds a cushion against a bad year; funding lower keeps rates down and leaves the
Pool exposed.

**Reinsurance, by line.** Whether to buy protection against large losses, and how much. Each option shows its yearly
price. Cover costs money every year; going without it leaves the Pool to absorb large losses on its own.

**Underwriting, by line.** How much existing business to renew and how much new business to accept. Being selective
can improve the quality of the book, at the cost of growth.

**Investments.** How the Pool's assets are divided among cash, bonds and equities, for the whole Pool.
Higher-return investments can also lose value in a bad year.

**Dividends and assessments.** Return surplus to members as a dividend, or ask them for an additional assessment. A
dividend lowers members' cost but thins the Pool's cushion; an assessment rebuilds the cushion but asks members for
more.

## Reading the reports

The Annual Statement shows the Pool's results year by year: premium, losses, expenses, investment income and
surplus, with the balance sheet beneath. View the Pool as a whole or one line at a time — a strong line can hide a
weak one.

Watch how surplus moves from year to year, and how much the Pool owes in claims against the cushion it holds. A
cushion that thins steadily is a warning, even if no single year looked bad.

Your departments report their best assessment each year. None of them is certain. Their input is a starting point;
the decisions are yours.
